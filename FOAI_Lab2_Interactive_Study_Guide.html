⚠️ Section 1: The Core Mental Model & Syntax Traps

Get these 5 rules in your head first so you don't lose marks on silly typing mistakes.

QUESTION 1 • THE #1 RULE

Why does Prolog care if I capitalize a word? What is the difference between john and John?

▼
💡 Think about this: In Python, x = "john" assigns a string to a variable. In Prolog, there is no assignment statement! How does Prolog distinguish between a concrete entity and an unknown search variable?

Prolog uses **capitalization** to distinguish between data and variables:

  • Lowercase (`john`, `pizza`, `cs101`) = Constant / Atom: This is a fixed value (concrete object, person, or relationship name).
  • UPPERCASE (`John`, `X`, `Person`, `Child`) = Variable: This is an unknown value that Prolog will try to solve for!
🚨 Fatal Exam Trap: If you write likes(John, Pizza). in your database, Prolog thinks you said "Anybody likes Anything"! Always write names in lowercase: likes(john, pizza).
QUESTION 2 • RULE OPERATORS

What does :- mean, and what is the difference between a Comma , and a Semicolon ;?

▼

In Prolog, rules are written as Horn Clauses: Head :- Body.

PROLOG RULE SYNTAX
father(F, C) :- parent(F, C), male(F).
  • :- means "IF" (The Head is true if the Body conditions are satisfied).
  • , (comma) means AND (Both conditions must be true).
  • ; (semicolon) means OR (Either condition is true). Tip: Instead of writing semicolons inside a rule, it is much cleaner to write two separate rules with the same name!
  • . (period) MUST end every fact, rule, and query.
QUESTION 3 • ARITHMETIC TRAP

Why does Total = Price1 + Price2 fail to calculate numbers, and why did Price <= 10 throw a syntax error?

▼
💡 Think about this: In Prolog, = does NOT mean "compute and assign". It means Unification (literal pattern matching).

If you write Total = 3 + 4, Prolog binds Total to the literal symbolic expression 3 + 4, NOT the number 7!

  • To calculate math: You MUST use the keyword is.
    Example: TotalCost is FoodPrice + DrinkPrice.
  • Less than or equal: In Prolog, it is written as =< (Arrow head on the right), NEVER <=.
    Example: Price =< Budget.
🚨 Fatal Exam Trap: Typing <= is the single most common error in student lab exams. SWI-Prolog will complain: Syntax error: Operator expected. Always type =<!
QUESTION 4 • NEGATION & MISSING INFO

What does \+ mean, and what is "Negation as Failure"?

▼

In First-Order Logic, something might be unknown. But Prolog operates under the **Closed World Assumption (CWA)**:

"If a statement cannot be proved true from the facts and rules in the knowledge base, Prolog assumes it is false."

The operator \+ means NOT (Negation as Failure).

EXAMPLE: FIND PARENTS WITH NO GRANDCHILDREN
% Find anyone X who is a parent of Y, but Y has NO children (_)
parent_of(X, Y), \+ parent_of(Y, _).
QUESTION 5 • CONSOLE BASICS

What do I do after typing a query? How do I get more answers or stop?

▼

When you run a query like ?- parent(john, Child)., Prolog will find the first match and pause:

  • Press ; (Semicolon) or Space: "Give me the next solution" (Prolog backtracks to search for more).
  • Press Enter: "I am satisfied, stop searching" (Returns you to the prompt ?-).
  • If your program gets stuck in an infinite loop: press Ctrl + C and then type a (abort).

QUESTION 7 • FATHER & MOTHER RULES

How do I write general rules for father(F, C) and mother(M, C)?

▼

Break it into logic: F is the father of C IF F is a parent of C AND F is male.

RULES FOR FATHER AND MOTHER
father(F, C) :- 
    parent(F, C), 
    male(F).

mother(M, C) :- 
    parent(M, C), 
    female(M).
QUESTION 8 • THE SIBLING TRAP

Why did Mary show up as her own sibling? How do I write the sibling rule correctly?

▼
💡 The Trap: If you write sibling(X, Y) :- parent(P, X), parent(P, Y)., what happens when X = mary and P = john? Well, John is the parent of Mary, and John is also the parent of Mary! So Prolog happily concludes Mary is her own sibling!

To prevent this, you **must explicitly add** X \= Y (X is not equal to Y):

CORRECT SIBLING RULE
sibling(X, Y) :- 
    parent(P, X), 
    parent(P, Y), 
    X \= Y.
QUESTION 9 • GRANDPARENT RULE

How do I write the rule for grandparent(GP, GC)?

▼

A grandparent is the parent of an intermediate parent P:

GRANDPARENT RULE
grandparent(GP, GC) :- 
    parent(GP, P), 
    parent(P, GC).

QUESTION 12 • AFFORDABLE RULE

How do I write affordable(Item, Budget) using the underscore _?

▼

When checking affordability, we only care about the item's name and price. We don't care about whether it's food or drink. Use the anonymous variable _!

AFFORDABLE RULE
affordable(Item, Budget) :- 
    item(Item, _, Price), 
    Price =< Budget.
QUESTION 13 • MEAL DEAL COMBO

How do I write meal_deal(Food, Drink, TotalCost) and find all combos ≤ $10?

▼
MEAL DEAL RULE
meal_deal(Food, Drink, TotalCost) :- 
    item(Food, food, FoodPrice), 
    item(Drink, drink, DrinkPrice), 
    TotalCost is FoodPrice + DrinkPrice.

QUESTION 14 • THE RECURSION FORMULA

What is the Universal 2-Clause Formula for all reachability and recursion in Prolog?

▼
💡 Memorize this single pattern: Every recursion question in this lab (Course Prereqs, Flight Paths, Ancestors) uses the EXACT same structure:
UNIVERSAL RECURSION FORMULA
% 1. BASE CASE: Direct 1-step connection
reach(From, To) :- 
    direct_step(From, To).

% 2. RECURSIVE CASE: Step to an Intermediate node, then recurse from there
reach(From, To) :- 
    direct_step(From, Intermediate), 
    reach(Intermediate, To).

QUESTION 16 • AIRPORT FLIGHTS

How does the Airport Flights problem use the exact same logic?

▼

Look at the code below: notice how has_route/2 is literally identical to requires_eventually/2!

AIRPORT FLIGHT RECURSION
% Direct route
direct_route(From, To) :- flight(_, From, To, _).

% Multi-stop route (Recursion)
has_route(From, To) :- 
    direct_route(From, To).

has_route(From, To) :- 
    direct_route(From, Via), 
    has_route(Via, To).
QUESTION 17 • INFINITE LOOPS

Why would requires_eventually(P, T) :- requires_eventually(P, I), prereq(I, T). crash?

▼
🚨 The "Left Recursion" Death Trap: Prolog evaluates subgoals strictly left-to-right. If the recursive call appears before taking a concrete step, Prolog calls itself with unbound variables forever without ever making progress! Always put the concrete fact/step prereq(Prereq, Intermediate) FIRST on the left.

🎖️ Section 5: Colonel West - Criminal Problem (`criminal.pl`)

Russell & Norvig's classic First-Order Logic to Horn clause inference proof.

QUESTION 18 • TRANSLATION TABLE

How do I translate the English narrative into First-Order Logic and Horn Clauses?

▼

This table shows the exact conversion from the textbook narrative into Prolog:

English Sentence First-Order Logic (FOL) Prolog Horn Clause
Colonel West is American American(West) american(west).
Nono is an enemy of America Enemy(Nono, America) enemy(nono, america).
Nono has missile m1 Missile(m1) ∧ Owns(Nono, m1) missile(m1). owns(nono, m1).
Missiles are weapons ∀x (Missile(x) ⇒ Weapon(x)) weapon(X) :- missile(X).
Enemy of America is hostile ∀x (Enemy(x, America) ⇒ Hostile(x)) hostile(X) :- enemy(X, america).
West sold all Nono's missiles ∀x (Missile(x) ∧ Owns(Nono, x) ⇒ Sells(West, x, Nono)) sells(west, X, nono) :- missile(X), owns(nono, X).
Selling weapons to hostile nation is a crime ∀x,y,z (American(x) ∧ Weapon(y) ∧ Sells(x,y,z) ∧ Hostile(z) ⇒ Criminal(x)) criminal(X) :- american(X), weapon(Y), sells(X, Y, Z), hostile(Z).
QUESTION 19 • COMPLETE CODE

What is the complete code for Colonel West?

▼
CRIMINAL PROBLEM (SAVE AS criminal.pl)
% Facts
american(west).
enemy(nono, america).
missile(m1).
owns(nono, m1).

% Rules
weapon(X) :- missile(X).
hostile(X) :- enemy(X, america).
sells(west, X, nono) :- missile(X), owns(nono, X).

criminal(X) :- 
    american(X), 
    weapon(Y), 
    sells(X, Y, Z), 
    hostile(Z).
QUESTION 20 • TRACING INFERENCE

How does trace. work, and what do the 4 ports (Call, Exit, Redo, Fail) mean?

▼

When you activate ?- trace. and run ?- criminal(west)., Prolog steps through the 4-Port Box Model:

  • Call: Prolog begins trying to satisfy a goal (e.g., Call: criminal(west)).
  • Exit: The goal was successfully proven and variables bound (e.g., Exit: weapon(m1)).
  • Redo: Prolog backtracks to search for an alternative solution.
  • Fail: No rule or fact could satisfy the goal.
EXACT TRACE OUTPUT FOR COLONEL WEST
1. Call: criminal(west)
2. Call: american(west)    --> Exit: true
3. Call: weapon(Y)         --> Exit: Y = m1
4. Call: sells(west, m1, Z) --> Exit: Z = nono
5. Call: hostile(nono)     --> Exit: true
6. Exit: criminal(west)    --> Returns: true.
